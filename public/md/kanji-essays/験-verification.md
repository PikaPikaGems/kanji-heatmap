# 験 (testing, experience, sign)

## What this kanji is, at its core

験 is the kanji for putting something to the test. It captures the moment you try something out to see what happens — an experiment, an exam, or simply the act of finding out. It also carries the idea of a sign or result that comes from that testing: the evidence that tells you it's real or it works.

## Common words that carry the 験's soul

### 試験 · しけん - examination / test

- This is the most common word for a formal test, especially in school.
- Everyday use: 試験に合格した · しけんにごうかくした — "I passed the exam."

### 実験 · じっけん - experiment

- This is the scientific side of 験: testing an idea to see if it’s true.
- Everyday use: 理科の実験をする · りかのじっけんをする — “I do a science experiment.”

### 経験 · けいけん - experience

- Here 験 points to something you've personally gone through and tested in real life, not just read about.
- Everyday use: いい経験になりました · いいけいけんになりました — “It was a good experience.”

### 体験 · たいけん - personal experience / hands-on experience

- Like 経験 but more personal — something you directly lived through with your own body and senses.
- Everyday use: いろいろな体験をしたい · いろいろなたいけんをしたい — “I want to have various experiences.”

## Where you'll see it in real life

School is the biggest one — 試験 (しけん, exam) is written on exam papers, study guides, and school calendars everywhere in Japan. In science class or a lab, you'll see 実験 (じっけん, experiment) on worksheets and equipment labels. In everyday conversation, people talk about 経験 (けいけん, experience) when discussing jobs, travel, or life lessons. You may also come across 霊験 (れいげん, miraculous power) in stories about shrines or old legends, where it refers to a god or charm actually "showing an effect."

## Component breakdown and mnemonics

**Component 1: 馬**

- Japanese name: うまへん. Literally "horse left." Why? へん means "left-side component," and うま means "horse" — it's the horse radical placed on the left side of a kanji.
- English name: horse
- Semantic: gives the kanji its original link to horses. Examples: 駅 (station — originally a post station where horses were changed), 駆ける (to gallop / run fast)

**Component 2: 㑒**

- Japanese name: not taught with a separate name in modern schooling; it's the simplified form of 僉.
- English name: unanimous, gather / all together. Many learners just call it "squid" as a nickname, since that's what the shape looks like.
- Semantic: means "many people gathering together," which also shows up in 検討 (けんとう, "consideration") and 検査 (けんさ, "inspection")
- Phonetic: けん/げん, as in 検討・けんとう ("consideration / study")

Some popular mnemonics to help you remember:

1.  A horse and a squid are put in a testing room together, make sure no one cheats.
2.  Many people 㑒 gather around a horse 馬 to test its speed

## Fun facts

The original form of this kanji was 驗. The Shuowen Jiezi defines it simply as “a horse’s name.” The meaning “test” or “verify” came later as a phonetic loan. Over time the testing sense completely took over, leaving the horse radical as a silent clue to its origins.

In Japanese Buddhism and Shugendō, 験 (げん) also means the visible proof of spiritual power—the miraculous effects of prayers or rituals—seen in words like 霊験 (れいげん) and 修験道 (しゅげんどう). Thus the same character links school exams and signs from the gods through the idea of something that proves itself through testing.

## Takeaway

験 is the kanji of putting something to the real test — a horse proving itself, an experiment proving a theory, a charm proving it works. Whether it's an exam, a lab result, or a lucky ritual before a big day, 験 is always asking the same question: did it actually hold up?
