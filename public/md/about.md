# About Kanji Heatmap

Our goal is simple: to be the best place on the web to learn about kanji.

## Kanji Heatmap is built for

- 🌱 Complete beginners wondering where to even start
- 🧗 Intermediate learners who keep mixing up look-alike kanji
- 🏔 Advanced learners who want to keep kanji they don't see as often from
  slipping away
- 📝 JLPT takers who want to study smart, not just hard
- 🎮 People who'd rather play their way to kanji than grind through lists
- ⏱ Anyone with short, scattered pockets of time who still wants to make
  steady progress
- 📱 People who'd like to swap some scrolling for learning that's actually fun
- 🤓 Kanji nerds who love geeking out over stroke order, parts and frequency
  data

## Explore and practice for free

**Everything about a kanji.** Click any kanji to see its readings, how to
write it, the parts it's built from, the words it appears in, and much more.

**Find the kanji you want, fast.** Search, sort and filter thousands of
kanji in an instant: by JLPT level, stroke count, frequency, and much more.

**Words worth learning.** We don't just list every word that uses a kanji.
We pick the ones worth knowing, based on popular word lists and frequency
data from 17 sources.
[Learn more →](#how-does-the-frequency-tier-system-work)

**A heatmap of what matters.** On the heatmap, the brighter a kanji, the
more often you'll see it; dimmer ones are rarer. You can switch between
several datasets, because social media, novels and TV dramas each favor
different kanji.

**Play your way to kanji.** Games for reading, writing and completing real
sentences, made to be fun and to make kanji stick.
([Why they work ↓](#the-kanji-heatmap-study-philosophy))

**AI study tools, if you want them.** Describe a scene that helps you
remember a kanji, and watch it come to life as a picture. Get a friendly
essay about any kanji or word, then listen to it. Stuck on a sentence? Ask
why it's built that way. Just bring your own AI key. It's all optional,
experimental, and off by default.

**Right on your home screen.** Add Kanji Heatmap to your home screen, and it
opens just like an app.

You don't need an account for any of these, and they'll always be free. More
free features are on the way.

**Bookmarks.** Bookmark kanji to mark the ones you already know, or to pick

**Natural-sounding audio.** Words are read in natural voices instead of your

## The Kanji Heatmap Study Philosophy

**You choose what to learn, and in what order.** We give you the
information to decide, not a fixed path. Already know some kanji? Skip
them. Not sure where to start? Many people find learning by JLPT level,
Jouyou grade, KKLC, RTK or WK order effective.

**Games that actually teach.** Decades of research show that pulling an
answer out of your own memory makes it stick far better than spotting it in
a list. Researchers call this _active recall_. So you type readings and
write kanji yourself. And when a game does give you choices, they're
look-alike kanji, so you learn to tell them apart.

**Think in Japanese.** Every kanji is paired with its own anchor word, and
no two kanji share one. If you can recall that word's reading, you've most
likely recalled its meaning too. You'll always see the meaning when you're
learning, but you never answer in English, so your memory stays anchored in
Japanese.

**Learn the kanji, not the font.** The same kanji can look quite different
from one font to another. Switch fonts anytime, or have practice show each
kanji in a random font, so you learn to recognize the character itself.

**Learn from real sentences.** We focus on recognizing kanji through real
words and sentences, not on their own. Language is picked up mostly by
reading and hearing things you understand, an idea from researchers like
Stephen Krashen and Beniko Mason. That's why there are tens of thousands of
sentences, with more on the way.

## Built with care

A good study app should feel effortless: nothing to wait for, nothing to
fight with. Getting there takes a lot of work you'll never see, and that's
the point.

Kanji Heatmap runs on your device first and syncs quietly in the
background. Search and filtering are instant, studying works on a train with
no signal, and you never stare at a loading spinner. It's the same approach
behind some of the fastest apps out there, and it's
[much harder to build than it sounds](https://linear.app/now/scaling-the-linear-sync-engine).

Then there are the little touches: type in romaji and it becomes kana as you
go, tap a Japanese word for its meaning, pick your own colors. On their own they're small, but together they add up. When
learning feels good, you keep coming back.

## FAQ

### Is Kanji Heatmap free?

Yes. Exploring kanji, the practice games and the AI tools (with your own
key) are free, and always will be. Premium adds reviews timed to your
memory, bookmarks, notes, progress tracking, natural voices and syncing
across devices.

### How does Kanji Heatmap know when I'm about to forget?

Premium uses FSRS, a scheduling method built from research on how memory
fades and tested on hundreds of millions of real reviews.
[Learn more](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition).

### How are anchor words selected?

Every kanji has a main anchor word: a common word that captures the core
idea of the kanji. Many kanji also have a second anchor word, another
common word that uses a common reading.

We pick them with our own scoring system. It weighs how common a word is
across 17 frequency lists, how many popular study lists include it, how
short it is, and how easy the other kanji in the word are. Words usually
written in kana, or spelled in a rare way, score lower.

You don't have to take our word for it: every anchor word shows its tags
and its rank in each of the 17 lists.

### How does the frequency tier system work?

Every word gets a score from its ranks in 17 frequency lists, covering
conversations, books and newspapers, the web, anime and dramas, YouTube,
and more.

The score is weighted, so each kind of Japanese counts equally. Six
anime-and-drama lists don't outvote the single list of real conversations,
and a word has to be common in more than one kind of Japanese to rank well.

Then the words are sorted by score:

- 🌱 Basic: the top 2,000
- ☘️ Common: the next 4,000
- 🌷 Fluent: the next 6,000
- 📚 Advanced: less frequent, but still widely used words
- 🌶️ Niche: words that don't fall into any of the other tiers
